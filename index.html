SautiFlow

AI audio skit studio · Content creation

SautiFlow turns a script into a finished skit: characters, voices, timing and sound effects generated from one prompt, so a creator can publish a daily sketch without booking anyone into a booth.

What it does

  • Script to skit — paste a premise or a full script and get a multi-character scene with distinct voices.

  • Character library — reusable voices with pitch, accent and delivery presets that stay consistent across episodes.

  • Automatic timing — pauses, overlaps and punch-line beats are placed on the timeline without manual editing.

  • Line-level re-takes — regenerate one line instead of re-rendering the whole scene.

  • Export presets — 15, 30 and 60 second cuts sized for Reels, Shorts and podcast drops.

How it works

  • Prompt expansion — a language model rewrites the premise into a speaker-labelled scene script.

  • Voice synthesis — each line is rendered by a multilingual TTS model conditioned on the character's voice embedding.

  • Scene mixing — rendered stems are laid on a timeline, loudness normalised and ducked under the sound bed.

  • Sound design — a tagged effects library is matched to scene cues such as laughter, ambience and transitions.

  • Delivery — the mix is rendered on a GPU worker, cached and returned as a shareable link.

Use cases

  • Sketch accounts that need volume without assembling a cast.

  • Podcasters turning written jokes into cold-open audio segments.

  • Brands localising the same ad script into several voices and languages.

PlusOne

AI personal photographer · Event imagery

PlusOne generates event-ready photographs from a handful of casual selfies, so a wedding, graduation or team offsite gets a full gallery without hiring a photographer.

What it does

  • One-time capture — upload four to six photos and get a persistent identity profile you reuse for every event.

  • Scene library — ceremony, family group, candid and studio templates, each with its own framing and lighting.

  • Batch albums — a whole album rendered overnight rather than one photo at a time.

  • Identity guard — generations that drift from the stored profile are rejected and re-rolled automatically.

  • Print and social exports — high-resolution masters plus cropped variants for feeds and prints.

How it works

  • Identity encoding — face embeddings and a per-person LoRA adapter capture the subject.

  • Composition — pose and framing references come from the selected event template.

  • Generation — a diffusion model renders the scene conditioned on identity plus prompt, then refines the face region.

  • Quality gate — similarity, sharpness and anatomy artefacts are scored, and failed frames never reach the album.

  • Upscale and delivery — frames are upscaled to print resolution and packaged as a shareable album.

Use cases

  • Weddings and engagements where guests want portraits that were never shot.

  • Corporate headshots for distributed teams without a studio visit.

  • Lookbooks and catalogue shots without booking models.

LabelSet

Data and labelling platform · African-first corpora

LabelSet sources, cleans and labels image, audio and video datasets built around African languages, accents and scenes, then hands them over in the layout your training code already expects.

What it does

  • Catalogue and search — browse ready-made corpora by language, region, scene and licence.

  • Managed labelling — transcription, bounding boxes, segmentation and speaker turns handled by a reviewer network.

  • Measurable quality — gold sets, inter-annotator agreement and per-annotator accuracy on every batch.

  • Versioned releases — content-hash versioning so a retrain reproduces the exact corpus.

  • Delivery — exports in COCO, LJSpeech or CVAT style layouts, or streamed straight into training.

How it works

  • Ingest — recordings and images land in a staging bucket and are de-duplicated by content hash.

  • Pre-label — ASR and detectors draft labels so annotators correct work instead of starting from blank.

  • Review — task schemas route each item through annotation and reviewer rounds, with disagreement flags.

  • Package — splits, consent records and dataset cards are attached to the release.

  • Serve — a dataset API issues signed download links for teams and training jobs.

Use cases

  • Teams training speech models on Kenyan, Swahili and other under-resourced languages.

  • Computer vision products that need locally representative imagery.

  • Research groups that need documented, consented corpora they can cite.

FaceForm

Synthetic portrait generator · Identity-safe imagery

FaceForm generates realistic Kenyan portraits for profile pictures, decks and campaign material, for the moments when the real person cannot be photographed.

What it does

  • Persona builder — set age range, region, styling and wardrobe, then generate faces that match the brief.

  • Style presets — studio headshot, environmental portrait, editorial and speaker-bio looks.

  • Export sizes — square avatar, 16:9 deck crop and print-ready portrait from a single generation.

  • Post-processing — face restoration, colour grading, then watermark and metadata tagging before download.

Use cases

Vocalis

Voice design and speech studio · TTS platform

Vocalis is a studio for designing brand voices and generating speech and sound from text — no recording booth, no voice actor booking, no re-shoots when the copy changes.

What it does

  • Voice design — describe age, pitch, accent and warmth and get candidate voices to audition.

  • Cloning from minutes — build a usable voice from a short, consented sample rather than hours of studio time.

  • Style presets — newsread, conversational, audiobook and ad-read delivery on the same voice.

  • Sound generation — effects and beds produced from a text prompt alongside the speech.

  • API access — the same voices served over REST for apps, IVR and e-learning players.

How it works

  • Guardrails — consent verification and audible watermarking before a cloned voice can be published.

  • Serving — a GPU inference queue with streamed output keeps first-byte latency low on long scripts.

Use cases

EchoiID

Speaker diarisation and transcription · Audio intelligence

EchoiID separates every voice in a recording and writes down exactly who said what, whether the file is hours old or still streaming in.

What it does

  • Diarisation — speaker turns segmented and colour-coded on the waveform.

  • Named speakers — enrol a team once and get names instead of "Speaker 2".

  • Transcription — per-speaker text with word timestamps and searchable history.

  • Live mode — partial results while the call is still running.

  • Export — SRT, VTT, JSON and document formats, plus a summary of who said what.

How it works

  • Voiceprint embeddings — short audio windows are turned into speaker embeddings.

  • Clustering — embeddings are grouped per speaker, with overlap handling for interruptions.

  • Transcription — diarised segments are transcribed and re-aligned to word-level timestamps.

  • Enrolment — known speakers are matched against stored embeddings to name the turns.

  • Serving — an async job queue handles files while a WebSocket stream serves live audio.

Use cases

  • Meeting notes and follow-ups split by participant.

  • Call-centre QA across long, multi-agent recordings.

  • Interviews and podcasts that need clean, attributed transcripts.
